Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Scalable Quantum Neighborhood
Search for the Permutation Flow Shop Problem via Windowed QUBO
Decomposition," for consideration as a research paper in Computers &
Operations Research.

Neighborhood-based metaheuristics remain the state of the art for the
permutation flow shop problem (PFSP). A recent line of work evaluates an
entire neighborhood in a single quantum-annealer call, but until now this
was confined to the smallest benchmark instances (n = 20 jobs). Our paper
removes that ceiling.

The main contributions are:

- Scaling from n = 20 to n = 500. We introduce an enhanced class of
  quantum-QUBO neighborhoods -- removing an unnecessary candidate filter
  from the linearly structured neighborhoods and decomposing the quadratic
  ones into overlapping windows -- that carries real quantum-annealer
  neighborhood search across the full Taillard benchmark range for the
  first time. Fibonacci Enhanced reaches parity with the classical
  baseline at n = 500.

- Results measured on real quantum hardware. Every quantum figure comes
  from a real D-Wave Advantage QPU under an instrumented protocol;
  nothing is simulated.

- A fair wall-clock benchmarking methodology. We report the anatomy of a
  single QPU call (billed access versus queueing and minor-embedding
  search), the single-iteration regime, and the stochasticity of
  embedding near the hardware capacity bound -- the measurements needed
  to compare quantum and classical neighborhood search honestly under
  time budgets.

The work fits the scope of Computers & Operations Research, which
publishes computational methods for operations research, including
metaheuristics, scheduling, and emerging quantum-optimization approaches.

For transparency: this study substantially extends our preliminary n = 20
investigation (currently under review as a separate short paper); the
present manuscript lifts the instance-size ceiling to n = 500 and shares
no results with it.
