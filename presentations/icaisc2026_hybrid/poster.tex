\begin{block}{Introduction}
\vspace*{3mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{Permutation Flow Shop Problem (PFSP)} schedules $n$
    jobs on $m$ machines in the same order; the goal is to minimise the
    makespan $C_{\max}$. It is NP-hard for $m\geq 3$.
  \item Performance of metaheuristics for the PFSP depends not only on
    the framework but, equally importantly, on the choice of the
    \textbf{neighborhood structure} used at each iteration.
  \item We compare three classical neighborhoods of different
    granularity --- Adjacent swap, the composite disjoint-adjacent
    neighborhood we call \emph{Fibonacci}, and the recursive
    Dynasearch --- inside Tabu Search (TS) and Simulated Annealing (SA).
  \item Experiments are conducted on the full Taillard benchmark with
    six time budgets between $100$\,ms and $10$\,s and the relative
    percentage deviation (PRD) of the obtained $C_{\max}$ to the
    best-known one as the performance metric.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{3mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{10mm}

\begin{block}{The Fibonacci neighborhood}
\vspace*{3mm}
A move is a subset $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n{-}1\}$ of adjacent-swap
positions satisfying the \textbf{non-overlap} constraint
\[
  i \in S \;\Longrightarrow\; i+1 \notin S,
\]
and \emph{all} selected swaps $\pi_i \leftrightarrow \pi_{i+1}$ are
applied \textbf{simultaneously}.

\vspace{4mm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.6]
  \foreach \x in {0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7} \fill (\x,0) circle (6pt)
      node[below=12pt,font=\Large] {\x};
  \draw[ultra thick,structure] (0,0) to[bend left=80] (1,0);
  \draw[ultra thick,structure] (2,0) to[bend left=80] (3,0);
  \draw[ultra thick,structure] (5,0) to[bend left=80] (6,0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{2mm}
The number of valid subsets in a permutation of length $n$ equals the
Fibonacci number $F_{n+1}$ --- hence the nickname. The \textbf{optimal}
subset (minimal $\sum_{i \in S}\Delta_i$) is found by a single linear-time
dynamic-programming pass given the precomputed $n{-}1$ adjacent-swap
deltas.

\vspace{3mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{10mm}

\begin{block}{Per-iteration cost}
\vspace*{3mm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Neighborhood} & \textbf{Candidates} & \textbf{Total per iter.} \\
\midrule
Adjacent                & $n-1$ swaps          & $O(mn)$   \\
\textbf{\emph{Fibonacci}}    & $n-1$ swaps $+$ DP   & $O(mn^2)$ \\
Dynasearch (rec.)       & $O(n^2)$ segments    & $O(mn^3)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{3mm}
The \emph{Fibonacci} neighborhood explores an \textbf{exponentially-sized}
set of moves ($F_{n+1}$ valid subsets) at \textbf{quadratic} per-iteration
cost --- the sweet spot between cheap Adjacent and expensive Dynasearch.
\vspace{3mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{10mm}

\begin{block}{Metaheuristic frameworks}
\vspace*{3mm}
\textbf{Tabu Search (TS).} Tabu list keyed by primitive moves or composite
signatures; tenure $\tau_{\text{tabu}}=10$; aspiration on global-best
improvement.

\vspace{3mm}
\textbf{Simulated Annealing (SA).} Multiplicative cooling
$T \leftarrow \alpha T$ with floor; \textbf{time-based reheating} kicks
in after $\tau_{\mathrm{stagn}}=500$\,ms of stagnation:
$T \leftarrow \min(T_0, r\cdot T)$ with $r=1.5$.

\vspace{3mm}
Both frameworks plug in the same set of neighborhoods, so any
performance gap is attributable to the neighborhood, not the
metaheuristic.
\vspace{3mm}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Experimental setup}
\vspace*{3mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Taillard instances, $n\in\{20,50,100,200,500\}$,
        $m\in\{5,10,20\}$ --- 12 instance classes.
  \item Time budgets:
        $tl\in\{100,500,1000,2000,5000,10000\}$\,ms.
  \item SA: $T_0=1000$, $T_{\mathrm{final}}=1$, $\alpha=0.95$,
        reheat $r=1.5$, stagnation 500\,ms.
  \item TS: tabu tenure $\tau_{\text{tabu}}=10$, aspiration on
        global-best improvement.
  \item Platform: MacBook Pro M4 Pro, 24\,GB RAM, Python 3.11.
  \item Metric: PRD to best-known $C_{\max}^{*}$,
        $\mathrm{PRD}=\tfrac{C_{\max}-C_{\max}^{*}}{C_{\max}^{*}}\cdot 100\%$.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{3mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{10mm}

\begin{block}{Conclusions}
\vspace*{3mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Three neighborhood structures (Adjacent, \emph{Fibonacci},
        Dynasearch) compared inside TS and SA on the full Taillard
        benchmark.
  \item The composite \emph{Fibonacci} neighborhood
        \textbf{dominates from $n=200$ onward}: at $n=200$ it improves
        over Adjacent by $0.48$\,pp and over Dynasearch by $0.49$\,pp at
        $tl=5\,$s; at $n=500$ it remains competitive while Dynasearch
        cannot complete a single iteration.
  \item Dynasearch reaches deeper optima at small $n$ but is impractical
        for $n\geq 200$ under short time budgets.
  \item SA's \textbf{time-based reheating} reliably escapes the plateau
        that the composite move induces once all $\Delta_i \geq 0$.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{3mm}
\end{block}
