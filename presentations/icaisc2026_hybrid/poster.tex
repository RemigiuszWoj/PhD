\begin{block}{Introduction}
\vspace*{2mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{Permutation Flow Shop Problem (PFSP)} schedules $n$
    jobs on $m$ machines in the same order; the goal is to minimise the
    makespan $C_{\max}$. It is NP-hard for $m\geq 3$.
  \item For metaheuristics, the choice of the \textbf{neighborhood
    structure} --- the set of moves explored each iteration --- matters
    as much as the framework itself.
  \item We compare three neighborhoods of increasing granularity inside
    \textbf{Tabu Search (TS)} and \textbf{Simulated Annealing (SA)}:
    the Adjacent swap, the composite \emph{Fibonacci} neighborhood, and
    the recursive Dynasearch.
  \item All under identical wall-clock budgets on the Taillard benchmark,
    measuring the relative percentage deviation (PRD) of $C_{\max}$ to
    the best-known value.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{7mm}

\begin{block}{Problem definition}
\vspace*{2mm}
Given a processing-time matrix $P=[p_{k,j}]_{m\times n}$, a permutation
$\pi$ fixes the job order. The completion times obey
\[
  C_{k,j} = \max\{C_{k,j-1},\,C_{k-1,j}\} + p_{k,\pi(j)},
\]
for $k=1,\dots,m$, $j=1,\dots,n$, with the objective
\[
  \min_{\pi}\; C_{\max}(\pi) = C_{m,n}(\pi).
\]
A \emph{neighborhood} of $\pi$ is the set of permutations reachable by a
specific admissible modification --- it governs how the search explores
and exploits the solution space.
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{7mm}

\begin{block}{Three neighborhood structures}
\vspace*{2mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Adjacent swap (baseline).} Swap positions $(i,i{+}1)$,
    $i=1,\dots,n{-}1$. Cheap to evaluate --- many iterations, but only
    shallow local moves.
  \item \textbf{Composite Disjoint-Adjacent (\emph{Fibonacci}).}
    A dynamic program selects a set of \emph{non-overlapping} adjacent
    swaps minimising the joint $\sum_i\Delta_i$ and applies them at once
    --- a bounded multi-step descent in a single iteration.
  \item \textbf{Dynasearch (recursive).} Considers all segments $[i,j]$,
    swapping their endpoints $\pi(i)\leftrightarrow\pi(j)$; a DP picks
    non-overlapping improving segments. $O(n^2)$ candidates --- deep but
    costly.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{1mm}
Related very-large-scale neighborhoods (six-operator~/ global-local)
trade breadth for per-iteration cost in the same spirit.
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{7mm}

\begin{block}{Per-iteration cost}
\vspace*{2mm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Neighborhood} & \textbf{Candidates} & \textbf{Total per iter.} \\
\midrule
Adjacent                  & $n-1$ swaps        & $O(mn)$   \\
\textbf{\emph{Fibonacci}} & $n-1$ swaps $+$ DP & $O(mn^2)$ \\
Dynasearch (rec.)         & $O(n^2)$ segments  & $O(mn^3)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace*{2mm}
\emph{Fibonacci} explores an \textbf{exponentially-sized} move set
($F_{n+1}$ valid subsets) at \textbf{quadratic} cost --- the sweet spot
between cheap Adjacent and expensive Dynasearch.
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{The Fibonacci move}
\vspace*{2mm}
A move is a subset $S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n{-}1\}$ of adjacent-swap
positions with the \textbf{non-overlap} constraint
\[
  i \in S \;\Longrightarrow\; i+1 \notin S,
\]
and all selected swaps $\pi_i \leftrightarrow \pi_{i+1}$ are applied
\textbf{simultaneously}.

\vspace{3mm}
\begin{center}

  \foreach \x in {0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7} \fill (\x,0) circle (6pt)
      node[below=12pt,font=\Large] {\x};

  \draw[ultra thick,structure] (5,0) to[bend left=80] (6,0);
  \node[above=8pt,structure,font=\large] at (0.5,0.5) {swap};
  \node[above=8pt,structure,font=\large] at (2.5,0.5) {swap};
  \node[above=8pt,structure,font=\large] at (5.5,0.5) {swap};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{2mm}
The number of valid subsets in a permutation of length $n$ equals the
Fibonacci number $F_{n+1}$ --- hence the nickname. The optimal subset is
found by a single linear-time DP pass over the precomputed swap deltas:
\[
  \mathrm{dp}[i] = \min\!\bigl(\mathrm{dp}[i+1],\ \Delta_i + \mathrm{dp}[i+2]\bigr).
\]
\vspace*{1mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{6mm}

\begin{block}{Metaheuristic frameworks}
\vspace*{2mm}
\textbf{Tabu Search (TS).} Tabu list keyed by primitive moves or
composite signatures; tenure $T_{\text{Tabu}}=10$; an aspiration
criterion accepts a tabu move when it improves the global best.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Simulated Annealing (SA).} Multiplicative cooling
$T\leftarrow\alpha T$ with floor and \textbf{time-based reheating}: after
$\tau_{\text{stagn}}$ ms of no improvement, $T\leftarrow\min(T_0,r\,T)$.
Standard acceptance:
\[
  \Pr(\textit{accept}) =
  \begin{cases}
    1 & \text{if } \Delta < 0,\\[1mm]
    \exp(-\Delta/T) & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Both frameworks plug in the same neighborhoods, so any performance gap
is attributable to the neighborhood, not the metaheuristic.
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Conclusions}
\vspace*{2mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Composite \emph{Fibonacci} accelerates \textbf{early}
    exploitation: a steeper initial $C_{\max}$ drop than Adjacent, by
    bundling non-overlapping improving swaps.
  \item It \textbf{dominates from $n=200$} under short-to-medium budgets,
    where Dynasearch cannot finish even one iteration ($\sim200\times$
    the budget at $n=500$).
  \item Once all $\Delta_i\geq 0$ the composite degenerates to a single
    swap and may plateau; \textbf{SA reheating} reliably restores
    diversification.
  \item Dynasearch remains best at small $n$ or with large budgets.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}

\vspace*{6mm}

\begin{block}{Future work}
\vspace*{2mm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Incremental delta evaluation} of the composite move,
    cutting the per-iteration cost from $O(mn^2)$ toward $O(mn)$.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive switching} between neighborhoods at runtime
    (e.g.\ Fibonacci early, Dynasearch once the budget allows).
  \item \textbf{Probabilistic variant sampling} of the swap subset for
    additional diversification.
  \item Integration into stronger solvers (iterated local search,
    variable neighborhood descent) and comparison with
    state-of-the-art FSP methods.
  \item \textbf{Quantum-assisted} Fibonacci selection on D-Wave Advantage
    (windowed QUBO, $n$ up to 500) --- companion paper at SOCO 2026.
\end{itemize}
\vspace*{2mm}
\end{block}
