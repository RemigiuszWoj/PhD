Quantum neighborhood search selects a scheduling move by sampling a QAOA circuit. We vary the number of QAOA layers p from 1 to 10 in four neighborhoods for the permutation flow shop problem with fixed angles, on Taillard instances with 20 jobs. Without noise, two layers almost always find the optimal move in the Dynasearch and Motzkin neighborhoods and beat one layer with 16 times as many shots. With simulated noise two layers are still best. On IQM Sirius, a physical superconducting quantum computer, extra layers bring no gain, and three layers make Dynasearch significantly worse. In simulation depth up to two layers is worth more than shots, but this gain does not carry over to Sirius.
