Evaluating a neighborhood on a gate-model quantum processor has so far been confined to the smallest scheduling instances, because the swap-selection QUBO needs one variable per candidate move and current devices hold a few dozen qubits. We remove that ceiling with a windowed decomposition that applies to all four neighborhoods used by the annealing line. For the one-hot Adjacent encoding, solving one window per position and keeping the best proposal returns the same argument of the minimum as the undecomposed problem. Every window of a move is submitted as one job, subject to a hundred-circuit cap that the provider reveals only on submission. The method reaches the entire Taillard benchmark on the sixteen physical qubits of the IQM sirius processor, and we report 225 runs covering all twelve configurations from twenty to five hundred jobs, to our knowledge the first measurement of gate-model neighborhoods beyond twenty. Deviation from the lower bound follows the ratio of machines to jobs more closely than either alone, and a paired comparison over 48 instances places the dense neighborhoods ahead of the linear ones in 38 of them. Against a classical loop on the same instances the margin falls from 8.7 to 1.1 as instances grow, because the classical search runs out of budget while the gate pipeline stays flat. Compared with the D-Wave annealer, the gate pipeline is the only quantum back-end that reaches every neighborhood at every size of the earlier scaling study, and it wins every dense cell the two share.
