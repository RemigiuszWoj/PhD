Quantum neighborhood search selects a scheduling move by sampling a QAOA circuit and keeping the best sample. Earlier gate-based neighborhood studies of the permutation flow shop problem used a single QAOA layer, and it is open how many layers such a neighborhood needs. We vary the number of layers p from 1 to 10 in four neighborhoods (Adjacent, Fibonacci, Dynasearch and Motzkin) with fixed angles. We measure how often the circuit samples the optimal move and how good the schedules found by iterated local search are on Taillard instances with 20 jobs. Without noise, one layer is too weak for Dynasearch and Motzkin, while two layers almost always find the optimal move and beat one layer with sixteen times as many shots. In Adjacent and Fibonacci no depth does significantly better than uniform sampling. With simulated hardware noise two layers are still best, because every further layer adds many two-qubit gates. We also run the search on IQM Sirius, a physical 16-qubit superconducting quantum computer accessed through the cloud. There, extra layers bring no gain, and three layers make Dynasearch significantly worse. In simulation depth up to two layers is therefore worth more than shots, but this gain does not carry over to Sirius. Beyond two layers the choice of angles matters more than the depth.
