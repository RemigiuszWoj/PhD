Quantum neighborhood search selects a scheduling move by sampling a QAOA circuit and keeping the lowest-energy sample. Earlier gate-based neighborhood studies of the permutation flow shop problem used one QAOA layer, and how many layers such a neighborhood needs is open. We vary the depth p from 1 to 10 in four neighborhoods (Adjacent, Fibonacci, Dynasearch and Motzkin) with fixed angles calibrated on three kinds of training windows. We measure the probability of sampling the optimal move and the makespan of iterated local search on Taillard instances with 20 jobs. Without noise, p = 1 is worse than uniform sampling in two neighborhoods. At p = 2 the best of 4096 samples is optimal in almost every window of Dynasearch and Motzkin, and with 256 shots the median deviation from the lower bound falls from 24% under uniform sampling to 15%, below one layer with 4096 shots. In Adjacent and Fibonacci no depth significantly improves the makespan over uniform sampling. On a simulated 16-qubit star processor with calibrated gate noise the hit rate of Dynasearch and Motzkin peaks at p = 2. Every further layer adds about 150 CZ gates and pushes it back toward uniform sampling. On the real processor, p = 1 does no better than uniform sampling, two layers bring no gain, and three make Dynasearch significantly worse. Beyond two layers the source of the angles matters more than the depth, and the mean energy minimized in calibration is a poor proxy for hitting the optimum.
