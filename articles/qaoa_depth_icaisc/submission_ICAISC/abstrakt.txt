Quantum neighborhood search selects a scheduling move by sampling a QAOA circuit and keeping the lowest-energy sample. Earlier gate-based studies of the permutation flow shop problem used a single QAOA layer, and it is not known how many layers such a neighborhood needs. We vary the depth p from 1 to 10 in four neighborhoods (Adjacent, Fibonacci, Dynasearch and Motzkin) with fixed angles calibrated on three kinds of training windows. We measure the effect on the probability of sampling the optimal move, on the circuit cost and on the makespan that iterated local search reaches on Taillard instances with 20 jobs. Without noise, p = 1 is worse than uniform sampling in two neighborhoods. Already at p = 2 the best of 4096 samples is optimal in almost every window of the dense neighborhoods, and with 256 shots the median deviation from the lower bound falls from about 24% to 15%. In the linear neighborhoods uniform sampling is as good as any depth. On a simulated 16-qubit star processor with calibrated noise the best depth of the dense neighborhoods is p = 2, and every further layer of about 150 two-qubit gates pushes the hit rate back towards uniform sampling. On the device itself, p = 1 behaves like uniform sampling, and two or three layers bring no gain. Beyond two layers the source of the angles matters more than the depth, and the mean energy minimized in calibration is a poor proxy for hitting the optimum.
